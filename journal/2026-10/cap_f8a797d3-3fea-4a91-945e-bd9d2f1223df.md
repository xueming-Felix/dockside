## Content

It's shorthand for: **every deadline in the freight day that costs or earns money is a timer inside the dock actor, not a human watching a clock.**

In a real dock day, several countdowns are running at once, and when each hits zero, money moves or is lost. Dockside makes each one a timer in the actor's timer table, backed by Terse's alarm, so the actor wakes itself at that minute and acts. Nobody has to be watching, there is no cron job, and it fires even when nothing is connected.

| The clock | Fires when | What the actor does on its own | The money on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detention | 2 hours after a truck arrived and is still waiting | Files a detention claim, grows it hourly | $75 an hour the warehouse pays the carrier |
| No-show | 30 minutes after a slot started with no truck | Releases the slot and opens an auction for it | A wasted door hour, unless re-sold |
| Auction close | A set number of minutes after a freed slot is posted | Closes bidding; earliest-ready truck is awarded the slot | Who gets the hour, and whose detention is avoided |
| Negotiation deadline | 10 sim minutes after a thread opens, if the agents haven't settled | Closes the thread; the standing offer applies | The side payment that never got agreed |

Scene 3 is the one where the room sees it: the auction closes with nobody pressing anything, and the "detention avoided" counter moves.

If the phrase feels too cryptic on stage, a plainer version: **"Every deadline that costs money is an alarm inside the dock. The dock wakes itself up; nobody watches the clock."**
